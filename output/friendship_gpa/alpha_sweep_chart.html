Katz–Bonacich centrality only diverges from raw popularity at high decay — and there, its independent effect on GPA turns negative

Sweeping the attenuation parameter α of Katz-Bonacich centrality from near-zero (α ≈ 0, equivalent to raw in-degree) up toward its theoretical convergence bound (α ≈ 1/λmax) shows where the measure stops being a relabeled popularity count — and that once it does, indirect network reach predicts lower GPA, holding popularity fixed.

At low α, Katz-Bonacich is numerically almost identical to in-degree (r = 0.9999) — so its apparent significance there (α=0.05, p=0.012) is just degree's own signal passing through a near-duplicate variable. Only once α approaches its theoretical ceiling does the measure genuinely capture multi-step, indirect reach through the network (r drops to 0.75) — and that is where an independent, robustly negative effect on GPA emerges (α=0.80: −0.048, p=0.031; α=0.95: −0.036, p=0.011). Betweenness centrality, a structurally distinct "bridge" measure, shows the same negative sign in the full horse race (−0.059, p<0.001).
